Batch Advisor Complaints workspace: three compositions

Grounded in DEC-283 (an actionable inbox with search, filters and complaint details; the cohort filter defaults to All Assigned Sections), DEC-221 (four statuses; a rejection reason and a resolution note are mandatory), DEC-222 ("Still not resolved"), DEC-223 (students add information), DEC-226 (no priority levels, queues or overdue indicator) and DEC-205 (exact cohorts). Sample advisor: Ms. Sana Iqbal, assigned to F24 · S4 · A F24 · S4 · B S25 · S2 · A. Search covers reference ID and student name within these cohorts only.

A · Split pane

An email-client workspace: the list on the left, the open case on the right, everything done in place. Selecting a complaint never leaves the page, so an advisor can move through their cohorts quickly. This is the "workspace, not dashboard" principle in its most familiar form.

Lanes don't fit a phone: they become status tabs. The board idea is lost here.

Hierarchy

  1. 1 Received and Under Review, where the work is, come first and grow.
  2. 2 Resolved and Rejected show only recent cases, so the terminal lanes don't grow forever.

Visual elements

  • Lane counts are the aggregate status journey: the advisor sees their workload's shape without a chart.
  • Flags (Reopened, New info) stand out on small cards.

Interactions

  • 3 Status changes use buttons in the drawer. No drag-and-drop between lanes, because resolving or rejecting requires a written note (DEC-221).
  • The drawer keeps the board visible behind it, so context is never lost.

Mobile

  • Becomes status tabs plus a list, which is essentially A's list. Phones get no board benefit.

Risks

  • Looks like a task board (Trello), which implies drag-to-move and personal workflow: neither applies here.
  • Lanes read as queues or priorities, which DEC-226 deliberately excludes.
  • With 20+ open cases, cards truncate heavily; less text per case than A.

C · Focus queue

One case at a time at a comfortable reading width, with the list reduced to a slim rail and a context column for the student's other complaints in the advisor's cohorts. Optimised for reading carefully and responding well, which matters for sensitive complaints. Moves through cases with next and previous, oldest first.

Hierarchy

  1. 1 "Waiting for you" is a view, not a priority: it lists cases with student activity since your last action, oldest first.
  2. 2 A slim rail keeps the position in the set visible.
  3. 3 One case at display size and reading width.
  4. 4 Context column: the student's other complaints and the cohort's representatives, all within the advisor's own cohorts.

Visual elements

  • The track shows a reopened journey honestly: resolved, then back to Under Review (DEC-222).
  • Keyboard hints are visible but small.

Interactions

  • J/K or the arrows move through the set; responding moves to the next case automatically (optional).
  • "All open" and "Closed" fall back to a normal list.

Mobile

  • The best phone experience of the three: one case per screen with next and previous.

Risks

  • "Waiting for you" could feel like a queue (DEC-226 rejected priority queues). It is a filtered view sorted by date, but the wording must avoid urgency.
  • Showing a student's other complaints is within permissions, but it should be checked against privacy expectations.
  • Weak for scanning or comparing many cases.
