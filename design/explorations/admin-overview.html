Super Admin Overview: three compositions

Grounded in DEC-284 (the Super Admin overview) and DEC-205 (exact cohorts). Sample Super Admin: Dr. Nadia Rehman. All figures are invented sample data.

Analytics scope: DEC-281 only Every composition shows only the three confirmed analytics: category share, distribution by section and resolved versus unresolved. No resolution or response times, satisfaction scores, trends, priorities, overdue counts, rankings or other metrics. Other areas (collective issues, calendar, people, configuration) appear only as navigation links, without numbers. Aggregates never name students (DEC-090).

Hierarchy

  1. 1 One sentence covering resolved vs unresolved and where the unresolved complaints sit.
  2. 2 Distribution by section as the largest element.
  3. 3 Resolved vs unresolved and 4 category share, each with a written takeaway.
  4. 5 Plain links into other areas, with no numbers.

Visual elements

  • Grey intensity, never red, so a high section reads as "look here", not "alarm".
  • Each cell shows its unresolved count and its total, which keeps shading honest for large and small sections.
  • One highlighted bar (the largest category), the rest muted.

Interactions

  • A cell opens that section's complaints, already filtered by exact cohort. A category bar opens complaints in that category.

Mobile

  • The map stays a grid, because three section columns fit at 360px. Category share collapses to its takeaway and opens full size.

Open questions

  • Rejected complaints: are they excluded from resolved vs unresolved, or counted as closed? The sample excludes them.
  • The map assumes each batch is in one semester at a time. If not, rows become batch + semester pairs.
  • The time window (current semester or all time) needs a decision.
  • Shading for very small sections, given small-group privacy (O25).

B · Written briefing

The overview as a short written report: three statements, one per confirmed analytic, each with the smallest visual that supports it and a link into the records behind it. The purest form of "say it before you show it", and the most editorial of the three. Distribution by section is drawn as a column strip in academic order, not a sorted ranking.

Hierarchy

  1. A dated greeting line, then the briefing as one card.
  2. 1 Resolved vs unresolved, 2 distribution by section, 3 category share, always in this order.
  3. 4 Navigation into other areas, as buttons without counts.

Visual elements

  • Large statement type is the page's character; each statement has exactly one small visual.
  • Sections in academic order with dashed batch dividers, so the strip shows distribution, not a league table.

Interactions

  • Each statement links to the filtered records behind it; each column opens that section's complaints.

Mobile

  • The best phone version: one statement per card, with its visual underneath.

Risks

  • Depends on well-written sentence rules: their order and wording become part of the specification.
  • The strip shows less structure than A's map (batch × section).
  • The same three statements every day can feel static on a quiet day.

C · Scope explorer

An oversight console: the department's structure as a tree on the left, with unresolved counts at every level (distribution by section, navigable), and the selected scope (department, batch or exact cohort) shown on the right with the same two remaining analytics. The Super Admin drills from the department to one cohort without leaving the page.

Hierarchy

  1. 1 The tree is distribution by section, made navigable.
  2. 2 The scope as the page heading, with its resolved vs unresolved counts.
  3. 3 Resolved vs unresolved, with the department figure for context, and 4 category share for the scope.
  4. 5 Links into the scope's records.

Visual elements

  • The same blocks at every scope, so the Super Admin learns one layout.
  • Comparing a scope with the department figure is context, not a ranking.

Interactions

  • Selecting a tree node changes the scope, and the URL, in place.

Mobile

  • The tree becomes a scope picker at the top; the blocks stack.

Risks

  • Overlaps with the Statistics page: the Overview could become a second statistics screen.
  • The department-level summary is only visible at the root of the tree.
  • A tree shows structure less vividly than A's map.
