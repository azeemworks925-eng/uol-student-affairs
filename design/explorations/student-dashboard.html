Student Dashboard: three compositions

Every version contains exactly the three confirmed sections (DEC-282): Your Actions (complaint updates, answered questions, meeting requests), Section Updates (active CR/GR issues awaiting support) and Quick Access (Submit Complaint, Student Voice, My Meetings). No statistics. Sample student: Ayesha Khan, F24 · S4 · A. All names are fictional.

Hierarchy

  1. 1 The summary sentence is the page's one display moment. It is generated from the same data as the list.
  2. 2 Quick Access as three buttons, Submit complaint the only Forest one.
  3. 3 Your actions: everything waiting on the student, newest first.
  4. 4 Your section: CR/GR issues with support.

Placement and visual elements

  • Desktop: 3:2 split, so the student's own items win.
  • Complaint rows carry a micro status track and the monospace reference ID.
  • Questions and meetings use a neutral tag; they have no status workflow of their own here (O29 for meeting statuses).

Interactions

  • Whole rows open the record. Support confirms inline and becomes "You support this", readable, not greyed out (DEC-241).
  • When nothing is waiting, the sentence says so: "Nothing needs your attention today."

Mobile

  • Same order, one column. The primary action sits directly under the sentence, full width.
  • Bottom navigation already reaches Voice and Meetings, so on phones those two buttons could be dropped; kept here for comparison.

Risks

  • The sentence must be written well for every data combination (0, 1, many); a weak generator turns it into noise.
  • Least visually distinctive of the three: its character rests almost entirely on type.

B · Case journeys first

The student's open complaints are the hero, each as a card with its full status journey and latest event. Everything else (answers, meetings, section issues) sits below. The most distinctive and most "product-like" version, built on the status-journey signature.

Hierarchy

  1. 1 A short count sentence, smaller than A's: the cards carry the story.
  2. Case cards with the full journey: the student's own complaints, then the most recently resolved one.
  3. 3 Other Your-Actions items, 4 section issues side by side.
  4. 5 Quick Access shrinks to links, since Submit complaint moved into the top bar.

Visual elements

  • Full status track with labels: the signature element at its largest.
  • 2 The resolved card offers "Still not resolved?", which returns the complaint to Under Review (DEC-222). Showing that here is a real benefit of this layout.

Interactions

  • Cards open the case timeline. At most three case cards; more opens My complaints.
  • Resolved cards leave the row after a while (the period is a decision to make).

Mobile

  • Cards stack full width. Deliberately not a carousel: no clipped, hidden cards.
  • The lower sections collapse to summary cards that open their lists.

Risks

  • Empty problem: most students have no open complaint most of the time. The hero then becomes an empty state, and the page loses its anchor.
  • Puts complaints above Student Voice and meetings, which DEC-282 treats as equal "actions".

C · You and your section

Two equal lanes: what is about you, and what is happening in your section. Quick Access becomes a single "raise something" launcher at the top. It makes the cohort a visible place and gives collective issues equal weight with personal ones.

Hierarchy

  1. 1 The launcher replaces three Quick Access tiles with one question and three answers.
  2. 2 "You" and 3 "Your section" as equal lanes with display-size headings.

Visual elements

  • Support counts as large numerals: the section lane reads like a noticeboard.
  • The cohort chip names the section the student belongs to.

Interactions

  • The launcher opens the right form directly. On phones it is one button opening a sheet.
  • On phones the lanes become a segmented control, so neither section is buried below the fold.

Mobile

  • Segmented "You · 3 / Section · 2" with counts, so the student sees both exist.

Risks

  • The launcher mixes two different processes: an identified complaint with a tracked lifecycle, and a private Student Voice message with no tracking ID (DEC-251). Students could pick the wrong one. Needs clear helper text, or a decision to keep them apart.
  • No summary sentence: a weaker match for the approved philosophy.
  • Equal lanes give collective issues as much space as personal items even when there are none.

Comparison and my recommendation

A · Headline and one listB · Case journeys firstC · You and your section
Philosophy fitStrongest: sentence first, type-ledStrong: journey signature at full sizeMedium: no summary sentence
DistinctivenessMediumHighestMedium-high
New student / empty stateGood: the sentence adaptsWeak: the hero is emptyFair: two empty lanes
DEC-282 balanceEvenFavours complaintsEven, plus a combined launcher
PhoneSimple and fastGood, long scrollGood, needs a tap to see the section

Recommendation for discussion: A as the base, with B's full journey card appearing only when a complaint has news (one hero card at the top of Your actions), and C's large support counts in Your section. That keeps the sentence-first calm for the common case and the journey signature for the moment that matters. I would not adopt C's combined launcher without a decision, because it blurs complaints and Student Voice.
